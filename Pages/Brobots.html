Brobots

2-Players Coop Fast FPS

Brobots is a 2-Player Coop Fast FPS, made in 4 month by a team of 17 students.
My main roles during this project were :

  • Balancing : Developing balancing tools, and working with Artists, Programmers and Game Designers to make sure the experience is fair but still challenging and satisfying
  • Game Design : Working with other Game Designers to create the concept, and iterate based on several rounds of feedback
My Experience
Since we were 5 Game Designers, each of us could focus more on a specific domain. For 4 month, I focused on Balancing. It was my first experience trying to Balance a multiplayer game, let alone an FPS. To make sure every part of the game was balanced and coherent, I worked closely with nearly everyone on the team. My work
In Balancing :

  • Coordinating with Programmers to develop tools, allowing for better Data Analysis and a smoother balancing pipeline
  • Coordinating with Artists to make sure the animations would be the right length and visuals would fit our needs
  • Integrating and testing values of different enemies, weapons and difficulties
On Brobots, the balancing was really challenging, and for several reasons.
The first one is obviously that I had never balanced an FPS, let alone a 2-Player Coop Fast FPS. This proved to be much harder than anticipated, especially because of some mistakes I've made :

The first balancing iteration I came up with on Brobots was way too focused on the details : I was trying to anticipate how hard the enemies would be to hit, and balance their HP and Damage based on that. However, guessing how hard enemies were to hit was just too hard and not needed.
Around half of the project, I started changing my logic, based on a few assumptions :
  • Guessing how hard enemies are to hit is useless since we do not want any enemy to be hard to hit
  • Balancing a single enemy is useless, players will never fight an enemy on 1 to 1 combat
  • As such, the behaviour of a group of enemies is more important than the behaviour of a single enemy
Overall, I started orienting my logic around the groups of enemies the players faces, rather than the specific enemy on that group : Who cares if a single enemy is too weak of too strong, as long as the fight against the group is fun ?

Changing this logic allowed me to balance enemies much faster, leaving more time to balance player abilities, and make their weapons satisfying.



In Game Design :

  • Working with the other Game Designers to design the base concept and mechanics
  • Organizing biweekly playtest sessions to validate our decisions
  • Iterating on our mechanics based on the feedback from playtests


Conclusion
In conclusion, Brobots was an interesting project for me, because it allowed me to explore further notions in Balancing and Game Design, as well as improving the key soft-skills needed to work with such a team.
