Non-official WoW Boss

Non-official WoW delve Boss, realized from scratch

The project
This is a boss I created from scratch. In my design, I wanted it to be the final boss of a new, hypothetical delve for the time-limited event "Midsummer Festival".
This boss (called "Krag Iceweaver"), is heavily affiliated with Ice, and that had to be something apparent in his abilities.
You can get more information about the Midsummer Festival here : Wowhead

Since I was alone on this project, I did everything on my own, but the roles I spent the most time on were :

  • Boss Design : Designing a boss coherent with its context, and making sure it is readable and fun to solve
  • Balancing : Making sure no class is overpowered, and the boss provides a sufficient difficulty while being rewarding
  • Tech GD : Prototyping an entire boss and a class system from scratch to test and validate my design
My Experience
The goal of this project was to create a World of Warcraft boss. Since I was alone on this project, I decided to pick only 3 specializations out of the 39 in the game at the time of this project and simplify them before programming them. I also decided to find a way to justify a single player boss in an MMORPG, simply because I just didn't know how to make networked games.
My work
In Boss Design :

  • Finding a context in which to place the boss that is coherent with what the game already does
  • Establishing coherent intentions for the boss based on the context it is placed in
  • Designing a set of interesting mechanics and interactions based on the intentions for the boss
  • Finding ways to make mechanics easy to understand, but keeping the boss as a whole a challenge to solve
For this boss, the main issue I had was the multiplayer : I was alone and had limited time, it was impossible for me to make a multiplayer boss.

But, how could you justify having a single player boss in World of Warcraft ? Moreover, I wanted this boss to test the player's skill, not be a simple quest boss.

To make sure my boss stayed coherent with what WoW already did, I turned towards the newest content at the time, Delves. Delves are short instances that can scale from 1 to 5 players, and they can easily get really challenging, meaning they were perfect for what I wanted to do.

I started designing the boss, using the principle of "Nemesis Delves", basically just encounters against a single boss in a Delve. I wanted this boss to be closely related to Ice magic, to better fit with the story I gave him. So, all of his attacks are designed using effects that Ice magic can already create in World of Warcraft.

I wanted every mechanic to have its own unique way of dealing damage :
  • One mechanic is a DoT Effect applied via a debuff from the boss.
  • One mechanic is applied by entering in contact with pillars in the room
  • One mechanic is a set of lines to avoid
  • One mechanic is a line that follows the player, who must avoid placing that line on pillars
  • One mechanic is a set of circles on the ground the player has to avoid
  • One mechanic is launching several ice blocks the player has to avoid
This choice meant that every action the boss could make would have a very distinct feedback, but a similar logic to the other actions the boss can make, making learning the boss significantly less about "what does the feedback mean ?" and more about "what should my reaction be" ?



In Balancing :

  • Using logging tools I've made to understand what is happening and balance the prototype based on real data
  • Creating Google Sheets tools to store all the values for the classes and the boss
  • Playtesting and organizing playtests to understand which parts need more tuning





In Technical Game Design :

  • Prototyping a simplified version of 3 World of Warcraft The War Within classes
  • Prototyping a modular attack and effect system for the player abilities and boss abilities
  • Prototyping an entire boss, with its movement and different phases and abilities
  • Programming a logging system, exporting data in .csv files, which can then be used for Data Analysis


Conclusion
That boss is still one of my favorite projects, even though it's not as polished as I'd want. It allowed me to work on my prototyping skills, as well as linking intentions and story directly into a boss. I also dabbled into some VFX for the feedbacks, which will come in handy when I need to make a new prototype.
